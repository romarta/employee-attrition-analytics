# Employee Experience — Findings

## 1. Analytical Objective

**Business Question: How do satisfaction and performance ratings differ between current employees and leavers?**

This analysis examines differences in employee satisfaction and performance ratings between current employees and employees recorded as leavers.

The analysis focuses on six dimensions:

- Job Satisfaction
- Work-Life Balance
- Environment Satisfaction
- Relationship Satisfaction
- Self-Rating
- Manager Rating

The objective is to identify descriptive differences between the two employee groups and determine which findings warrant further statistical investigation.

The analysis is cross-sectional and exploratory. It does not establish causal relationships or confirm that the selected ratings preceded employee departures.

---

## 2. Analytical Population

Two employee populations are distinguished throughout the project.

| Population | Employees | Current Employees | Leavers | Attrition Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Full Workforce | 1,470 | 1,233 | 237 | 16.1% |
| Eligible Review Cohort | 1,217 | 993 | 224 | 18.4% |

The Employee Experience analysis uses the **Eligible Review Cohort**, representing 82.8% of the full workforce.

### Review Eligibility Criteria

The original performance dataset contained 6,709 review records.

During data quality assessment, 1,141 reviews were identified with dates preceding employee hire dates.

These records were excluded from review-based analysis.

The remaining 5,568 chronologically eligible reviews were used to identify the most recent eligible review for each employee.

This process resulted in:

- 1,217 employees with eligible reviews;
- 993 current employees;
- 224 employees recorded as leavers.

A total of 253 employees had no eligible review and were excluded from review-based analysis.

These employees remain included in analyses based on the full workforce.

### Review Coverage

| Employee Status | Eligible Reviews Coverage |
|---|---:|
| Current Employees | 80.5% |
| Leavers | 94.5% |
| Overall | 82.8% |

Review availability differs between the two groups.

Consequently, the eligible-review cohort contains a proportionally higher share of leavers than the full workforce.

This introduces a potential **selection bias** and limits the direct generalizability of review-based findings.

## 3. Satisfaction Scores by Attrition Status

Four satisfaction dimensions were analyzed using the latest eligible review for each employee.

Each dimension is recorded on an ordered five-point scale.

### Average Satisfaction Scores

| Satisfaction Aspect | Current Employees | Leavers | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Environment Satisfaction | 3.72 | 3.67 | -0.05 |
| Work-Life Balance | 3.40 | 3.27 | **-0.13** |
| Job Satisfaction | 3.38 | 3.27 | **-0.11** |
| Relationship Satisfaction | 3.33 | 3.30 | -0.03 |

*Difference = Leavers − Current Employees.*

### Finding 1 — Leavers Report Slightly Lower Satisfaction Scores

Average satisfaction scores are slightly lower among leavers across all four dimensions.

The largest descriptive differences are observed in:

1. Work-Life Balance: -0.13 points.
2. Job Satisfaction: -0.11 points.

Differences in Environment Satisfaction and Relationship Satisfaction are smaller.

However, all observed differences are modest relative to the five-point rating scale.

These findings should not be interpreted as evidence that lower satisfaction caused employee departures.

## 4. Satisfaction Rating Distributions

Average scores were supplemented with category-level distributions.

This is important because satisfaction ratings are ordinal, and similar averages may conceal differences in the distribution of individual responses.

### 4.1 Complete Distribution Counts

| Satisfaction Aspect | Attrition | Rating 1 | Rating 2 | Rating 3 | Rating 4 | Rating 5 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Environment Satisfaction | No | 40 | 45 | 328 | 319 | 261 |
| Environment Satisfaction | Yes | 9 | 16 | 76 | 61 | 62 |
| Work-Life Balance | No | 29 | 242 | 233 | 281 | 208 |
| Work-Life Balance | Yes | 13 | 54 | 61 | 52 | 44 |
| Job Satisfaction | No | 33 | 237 | 253 | 259 | 211 |
| Job Satisfaction | Yes | 12 | 48 | 69 | 58 | 37 |
| Relationship Satisfaction | No | 39 | 267 | 218 | 262 | 207 |
| Relationship Satisfaction | Yes | 10 | 51 | 66 | 55 | 42 |

For each satisfaction aspect, the responses total 993 current employees and 224 leavers.

### 4.2 Low and High Rating Shares

To facilitate descriptive comparisons, ratings were grouped into two categories:

- Ratings 1–2: lower end of the scale.
- Ratings 4–5: higher end of the scale.

These categories are descriptive groupings rather than validated thresholds.

| Satisfaction Aspect | Ratings 1–2 Current | Ratings 1–2 Leavers | Ratings 4–5 Current | Ratings 4–5 Leavers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Environment Satisfaction | 8.6% | 11.2% | 58.4% | 54.9% |
| Work-Life Balance | 27.3% | 29.9% | 49.2% | 42.9% |
| Job Satisfaction | 27.2% | 26.8% | 47.3% | 42.4% |
| Relationship Satisfaction | 30.8% | 27.2% | 47.2% | 43.3% |

### Finding 2 — Work-Life Balance Shows the Largest Difference in High Ratings

Among current employees, 49.2% of Work-Life Balance responses fall into the two highest categories.

Among leavers, the corresponding share is 42.9%.

The difference is approximately **6.4 percentage points**.

Lower ratings (1–2) are also slightly more common among leavers:

- Current Employees: 27.3%.
- Leavers: 29.9%.

This represents the clearest descriptive difference among the four satisfaction dimensions.

### Finding 3 — Job Satisfaction Differences Are Concentrated in Higher Ratings

The proportion of Job Satisfaction ratings 1–2 is nearly identical:

- Current Employees: 27.2%.
- Leavers: 26.8%.

However, the share of ratings 4–5 is lower among leavers:

- Current Employees: 47.3%.
- Leavers: 42.4%.

This suggests that the descriptive difference in Job Satisfaction is associated more with a reduced proportion of high ratings than with a substantially greater proportion of the lowest ratings.

### Finding 4 — Environment and Relationship Satisfaction Are Broadly Similar

Environment Satisfaction and Relationship Satisfaction show relatively small differences between the groups.

For Relationship Satisfaction, the share of ratings 1–2 is actually lower among leavers than among current employees.

This illustrates why average scores should not be interpreted without examining the underlying distributions.

## 5. Performance Rating Comparison

Performance ratings were analyzed using each employee's latest eligible review.

Two measures were considered:

- SelfRating — employee self-assessment.
- ManagerRating — assessment provided by the employee's manager.

### 5.1 Average Performance Ratings

| Performance Measure | Current Employees | Leavers |
|---|---:|---:|
| Average Self-Rating | 3.96 | 3.92 |
| Average Manager Rating | 3.46 | 3.42 |

The differences are approximately 0.04 points for both measures.

### 5.2 Self-Rating Distribution

| SelfRating | Current Employees | Leavers |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3 | 343 | 83 |
| 4 | 348 | 75 |
| 5 | 302 | 66 |
| **Total** | **993** | **224** |

The distribution is similar across both groups.

Ratings of 3 account for:

- 34.5% of current employees;
- 37.1% of leavers.

Ratings of 5 account for:

- 30.4% of current employees;
- 29.5% of leavers.

### 5.3 Manager Rating Distribution

| ManagerRating | Current Employees | Leavers |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2 | 177 | 50 |
| 3 | 345 | 65 |
| 4 | 311 | 75 |
| 5 | 160 | 34 |
| **Total** | **993** | **224** |

Employees recorded as leavers have a slightly higher share of ManagerRating = 2:

- Current Employees: 17.8%.
- Leavers: 22.3%.

However, ManagerRating = 4 is also slightly more common among leavers.

The overall distributions therefore do not show a consistent shift toward lower manager ratings.

### Finding 5 — Performance Ratings Do Not Clearly Distinguish the Groups

SelfRating and ManagerRating exhibit relatively small descriptive differences between current employees and leavers.

The observed distributions overlap substantially.

Consequently, the available results do not support the interpretation that employees who left the organization consistently had lower recorded performance ratings.

This finding does not establish that performance is statistically unrelated to attrition.

### Sales

Leavers have slightly lower average ratings in three dimensions:

- Environment Satisfaction;
- Work-Life Balance;
- Job Satisfaction.

The largest difference is observed in Work-Life Balance (-0.19 points).

This is consistent with the overall descriptive pattern.

### Human Resources

The direction of the differences is mixed.

Leavers have lower average Environment Satisfaction and Work-Life Balance ratings, but higher Job Satisfaction and Relationship Satisfaction ratings.

However, only **12 leavers** with eligible reviews are represented in Human Resources.

Consequently, these subgroup comparisons must be treated cautiously.

### Technology

The differences are generally small.

The largest negative difference is observed in Job Satisfaction (-0.12 points).

Environment Satisfaction is slightly higher among leavers (+0.05 points).

### Finding 6 — Employee Experience Patterns Differ Across Departments

The observed relationships are not identical across the organization.

This suggests that organizational context may be relevant when interpreting employee experience.

However, subgroup differences should not be treated as evidence of statistically significant interactions without further analysis.

## 8. Consolidated Findings

The Employee Experience analysis produced the following main observations:

**Work-Life Balance has the largest descriptive mean difference.**

It has the largest overall mean difference between leavers and current employees (-0.13 points).

**Finding 2 — Leavers are less represented among high Work-Life Balance and Job Satisfaction ratings.**

The proportion of ratings 4–5 is lower among leavers in both dimensions.

**Finding 3 — Satisfaction differences vary across departments.**

The direction and magnitude of descriptive differences are not uniform across Sales, Human Resources and Technology.

**Finding 4 — Performance ratings show limited differences between the two groups.**

SelfRating and ManagerRating distributions are broadly similar at the overall cohort level.

**Finding 5 — Human Resources findings require particular caution.**

Only 12 leavers with eligible reviews are included in this department.

**Finding 6 — Work-Life Balance and overtime form an exploratory business hypothesis.**

Earlier analyses identified higher recorded attrition shares among employees with overtime.

The Employee Experience analysis additionally found slightly lower average Work-Life Balance ratings among leavers.

These observations suggest a potential area for further investigation.

However, the available data do not establish that overtime reduced Work-Life Balance or that lower Work-Life Balance caused departures.

## 9. Analytical Limitations

### 9.1 Review Chronology

The selected reviews satisfy the condition:

`ReviewDate >= HireDate`

However, employee termination dates are unavailable.

It is therefore impossible to confirm that the selected reviews preceded employee departures.

The ratings must not be interpreted as verified pre-attrition indicators.

### 9.2 Selection Bias

The analysis includes only employees with eligible reviews.

Review coverage differs between current employees and leavers.

Consequently, the reviewed population may not be fully representative of the workforce.

### 9.3 Ordinal Rating Scales

Satisfaction and performance ratings are ordinal measures.

Although average scores provide useful descriptive summaries, they assume numerical spacing between categories.

For this reason, conclusions are based on both average scores and full category distributions.

### 9.4 Small Subgroups

Human Resources contains only 12 reviewed leavers.

Department-level differences involving this group may be unstable and should not independently determine business recommendations.

### 9.5 Cross-Sectional Analysis

The analysis describes associations between recorded employee characteristics and attrition status.

It does not establish temporal order, causality or predictive performance.

### 9.6 Statistical Validation

The descriptive findings were subsequently evaluated using statistical methods in Python.

Mann–Whitney U tests were applied to compare satisfaction and performance ratings between current employees and recorded leavers. The satisfaction comparisons were adjusted for multiple testing using the Holm method.

None of the four satisfaction dimensions or the two performance measures showed statistically significant differences between the groups.

The findings remain exploratory because review availability differs by attrition status, and the timing of reviews relative to employee departures cannot be verified.

## 11. Implications for Retention Strategy

The analysis suggests several areas worth investigating further:

**Workload and Work-Life Balance**

Given the previously observed association between overtime and attrition, Work-Life Balance may be an appropriate topic for qualitative employee research and workload assessment.

**Employee Satisfaction Monitoring**

The organization could consider examining satisfaction patterns more systematically, particularly across departments and job roles.

**Performance Ratings**

The similarity of performance-rating distributions suggests that recorded performance levels alone would provide a limited basis for distinguishing employees who left from those who remained.

These are exploratory implications, not evidence that specific interventions will reduce future attrition.

Any retention recommendations should be evaluated using additional organizational context and, where possible, prospective evidence.
